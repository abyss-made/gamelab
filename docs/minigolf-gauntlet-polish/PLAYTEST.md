# Human playtest handoff

**No human playtest observations have been collected in this environment.** Browser automation and control-noise simulations verify mechanics and expose defects; they do not establish that real people find a course fun or intuitive.

Use two first-time players for one six-hole match, then repeat a few holes with four to six players. Include one 390px-class phone and one desktop. Start with the normal eight-stroke limit. Avoid coaching beyond the instructions already shown in the game.

| Check | Human observation to record |
| --- | --- |
| Understand the first shot | Can each player start within 15 seconds using the visible instructions? |
| Read the machine | Before putting, can they explain when the sweeper moves and identify its danger area? |
| Aim on a phone | Does the ball stay under the finger? Is power easy to judge? Can they find precise Aim controls? |
| Choose a route | Do they recognize a safe bypass and a risky shortcut? |
| Read an impact | Can they tell whether a wall, bumper, another ball or a sweeper caused the deflection? |
| Recover from slime | Is the +1 penalty understandable and the restored position fair? |
| Finish a hole | Does the cup/score feedback feel satisfying without delaying the next turn? |
| Play with friends | Are turns, player balls and rankings easy to follow with six players? |
| Cancel or reconnect | Can a cancelled gesture avoid shooting? Does reconnecting preserve the seat and score? |

Record the branch/commit, device, browser, player count, per-hole strokes, confusing moments and any covered control. Ask each player for their favorite moment, most frustrating moment, and whether they would play another round. Leave observations blank until somebody actually plays; prioritize their findings over an automated score.
